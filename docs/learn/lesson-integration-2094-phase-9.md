## Result

Nine Phase 9 lessons were learner-sufficient before changes. Lesson 81 had one source-dependent gap: the normal lesson named major film organizations but did not explain enough of their distinct functions for a learner to understand why the examples differ without opening the source viewer.

### Lesson 81 — The Film Industry

The normal lesson already taught the durable category model: labour organizations, professional associations, cultural institutions, funders, markets and jurisdiction all do different jobs, and live rates, eligibility, agreements and membership rules must be verified from current official sources.

Phase 9 adds one concise learner-facing section, `What the major organizations actually do`, plus definitions for `Trade association` and `Professional society or academy`. The added teaching explains stable role distinctions for WGA, DGA, SAG-AFTRA, IATSE, PGA, AMPAS, ASC, ACE, BFI, BAFTA, EFA, FERA, Cinémathèque Française, MPA/exhibition trade associations and VES.

The integration deliberately does **not** freeze membership counts, current rosters, rates, eligibility, benefit schedules, agreement terms or other live organizational facts. It also does not promote inaccurate historical claims such as treating PGA as the labour-union equivalent of WGA/DGA/SAG-AFTRA. Those details remain historical provenance or require current authoritative verification.

### Lesson 82 — Ownership, Licences and Sharing Choices

The lesson already distinguishes copyright, licences, access, publication, reuse, credit, provenance, third-party material and professional-review boundaries. Its attached `Copywriting Formulas` source is not ownership teaching. Durable audience-language material belongs to the existing pitch/audience-language curriculum; generic formulas and old AI prompt framing remain historical rather than being copied into this lesson.

### Lesson 83 — Choose Your PlotPickle Workflow

The lesson already teaches the smallest-fit workflow: fully local, file exchange, optional bounded AI, private version control, and public/open publishing as separate choices. Its ten historical sources reinforce optional version control, open/public distinctions, centralized/decentralized workflow choices, defined collaborator roles and shared structured story language. GitHub setup instructions, promotional platform claims, Web3/token visions, obsolete product framing and claims that open publication inherently produces visibility or access remain historical or rejected. Rights depth belongs to Lesson 82 and later collaboration-rights teaching.

### Lesson 84 — Collaborate Without Losing the Source of Truth

The lesson already separates owner, writer, reviewer and contributor roles; keeps proposals distinct from canon; uses stable anchors and before/after comparison; and records attribution and approval. The three attached sources support communication, reviewable proposals and explicit roles. GitHub-specific instructions, open-source advocacy and historical AI-role hierarchies are not promoted as mandatory workflow authority. Version history is not treated as an ownership agreement.

### Lesson 85 — Formats That Travel

The lesson already distinguishes complete project exchange, screenplay interchange, reading formats and documentation/presentation formats, and explicitly replaces old Final Draft-to-text workarounds with direct FDX/Fountain interchange plus comparison after round trips. The Markdown tutorial and WriterDuet-era workaround remain historical details; Markdown is not promoted as screenplay formatting and no round trip is assumed lossless.

### Lesson 86 — Choose the Collaboration Model

The lesson already defines ownership, access, proposal rights, canon authority, privacy, credit, compensation and licensing across several valid collaboration models. The attached Afterglow contribution policy is useful provenance for bounded contribution, rights and explicit acceptance, but its project-specific rules and temporary licence conflict do not become general PlotPickle teaching.

### Lesson 87 — Define Roles and Decision Authority

The lesson already separates creative authority from technical permission and provides an explicit authority matrix. Historical Twitter/GitHub/AI experimentation remains provenance. Licensing/protection detail is owned by Lesson 82 and later rights teaching; claims that a licence guarantees protection, compensation, attribution or practical control are rejected.

### Lessons 88–90

`Create a Contribution Brief`, `Start From the Approved Story` and `Submit a Reviewable Proposal` are source-free and already teach bounded scope, locks, acceptance criteria, approved-base handling, stale-base reconsideration, proposal evidence, dependencies, provenance and explicit review before canon changes. No source-driven rewrite is justified.
