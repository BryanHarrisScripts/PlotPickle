# Story Revision Review

Use for the existing continuity/revision role on a bounded approved scene, passage, character profile or sequence. This native procedure adapts editorial-review, voice-style, reader-panel and screenwriting dialogue review ideas without executing an external plug-in.

## Procedure

1. Establish the writer's requested pass: continuity-only, dialogue/voice, revision diagnosis or simulated reader response. Keep the input revision ID and the approved context scope.
2. Mark source evidence separately from inferred audience reactions. Identify any contradiction by the two specific conflicting statements, character knowledge states and ordering of events.
3. For voice, compare vocabulary, syntax length, status, hesitation, silence, subtext and the character's physical objective to their approved voiceprint; cite actual lines rather than generic advice.
4. For revision diagnosis, test each scene's entry state, objective, obstacle, turn, exit state and handoff. Return symptom -> evidence -> possible cause -> minimum proposed repair.
5. For reader response, label the reaction simulated, list which context the hypothetical reader saw, state at most three plausible interpretations and the evidence behind each. Do not fabricate an audience survey, real readership, market validation or a consensus.
6. Give up to three alternatives only when the writer requested alternatives. Present original beside each proposal and flag new canon, continuity, research or consent decisions for Human review.
7. After writer selection, ask the host to preserve the accepted revision lineage. This skill itself cannot write, approve or overwrite anything.

## Failure and stop conditions

- Missing approved voiceprint: say so; do not invent one.
- Missing previous shot/frame or unknown scene chronology: report the unresolved comparison.
- A request to auto-accept an editorial fix: return a reviewable suggestion, not a state mutation.
- Unsupported legal, medical or culturally specific claims: separate fact-check work from creative option generation.

## Learner handoff

Ground explanations in current PlotPickle LEARN dialogue and revision lessons. Demonstrate on a fictional two-line sample, offer a small practice exercise and let the writer apply the finding. Feedback is advisory, not evaluation of the student.

Host owns PPF, persistence, permissions, provider/budget and independent verification. No external script execution, provider selection, credentials or direct changes to accepted story, shots or lesson completion.
