## Result

All ten Phase 6 lessons were learner-sufficient before changes. No learner-facing curriculum rewrite was justified.

### Drafting

`The Pickle Draft` already carries the useful rough-drafting principle from the historical “vomit draft” source: move forward, postpone local polish, use explicit markers for unknowns, preserve causality and turn the completed rough draft into evidence for revision. The legacy label and motivational framing remain historical rather than mandatory terminology.

`Script Formatting` already teaches screenplay format as readable production language: scene headings, visible present-tense action, character cues, dialogue, parentheticals, meaningful transitions, plus portable Fountain/FDX/PDF workflows. Static software recommendations and page-count-as-runtime assumptions are not promoted as permanent rules.

`Books, Screenplays and Deliberate Study` already converts the old resource-list source into a durable study method: read legally available scripts for decisions, identify document type, compare page and finished scene, and turn examples into deliberate practice without copying expression. Historical book rankings and recommendation lists remain provenance only.

`Screenplay Challenges Guide` already traces visible symptoms to the underlying story, character, scene, dialogue, process, continuity, research or practical layer and asks for bounded experiments instead of generic fixes. Revision-specific repair sequencing remains owned by the Revision curriculum.

`Write Scenes That Change Conditions` already teaches entry condition, indispensable purpose, objective, opposition, tactic, pressure, pivot, value change, consequence, exit and handoff through Scene Pulse evidence. Line-level dialogue craft remains owned by Dialogue.

`Use Advanced Screenplay Forms Clearly` already teaches secondary headings, montage, series of shots, intercutting, remote conversations, V.O./O.S., dual dialogue, inserts and transitions by dramatic purpose and readable export behaviour rather than decoration.

`Scene Craft: Objective, Pressure and Turn` is an independently authored source-free enrichment lesson. It already teaches entry condition, playable objective, stakes, knowledge, pressure, changing tactics, visible carriers, meaningful turns, exit conditions and handoffs while explicitly treating the pattern as diagnostic rather than a mandatory scene formula.

`Screenplay Format and Delivery` is also source-free enrichment. It already distinguishes durable readability conventions from document purpose, production metadata, export inspection and current recipient-specific delivery requirements. Time-sensitive competition, guild, employer or production rules remain outside frozen curriculum authority.

### Dialogue

`Dialogue and Voiceprint pass` is intentionally a narrow guided pass. It already establishes bounded scope, reviewable alternatives, preservation of facts/intent and explicit human approval. The attached legacy Dialogue prompt and repository overview contain broader action, subtext, conflict, exposition and revision teaching that is owned by the dedicated Dialogue lessons rather than duplicated here. The old prompt wrapper and repository navigation remain historical only.

`Subtext, status and silence pass` is source-free and already teaches the intended narrow operation: identify on-the-nose speech, static status and inactive silence; keep suggestions bounded; preserve writer intent and require explicit approval.
