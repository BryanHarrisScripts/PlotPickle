# #2094 fidelity audit — Lessons 31–40

This batch applies the stricter fidelity rule to Theme / Motif and the original 24 Blocks structure material. The governing change is that older prescriptive frameworks are no longer discarded merely because PlotPickle does not enforce them. Durable framework vocabulary is preserved as optional reference material, while current PlotPickle guidance remains non-prescriptive.

## 31 — Build Motifs, Symbols and Echoes

- `PRESERVED`: motif, symbol, metaphor, visual/sonic rhyme, foreshadowing, setup/payoff, dramatic irony, allegorical design and opening/closing echo remain primary.
- `PRESERVED`: source techniques should remain explicit rather than compressed into one list: repeated symbolic actions, colour, symbolic sound/music, objects, metaphoric setting, symbolic dialogue, symbolic names, mirror characters, allegory and irony.
- `MOVED_ELSEWHERE`: dialogue subtext remains owned by Dialogue; broad visual storytelling remains owned by Visual Storytelling; mirror-character craft also connects to Opposition / Cast.
- `REJECTED`: fixed symbol dictionaries and universal colour/object meanings.
- Formatting target: one advanced-symbolic-toolkit section with short explanatory bullets.

## 32 — Story Structures: Screenplays to Improv

- `PRESERVED`: three-act, five-act, sequence/seven-act, Hero's Journey, nonlinear, parallel, circular, framed and episodic forms remain explicit.
- `PRESERVED`: the source's cross-medium distinctions are durable and should remain learner-visible: stage, prose, poetry, audio, comics/graphic novels, games, web series, music-led forms, animation and nonfiction.
- `PRESERVED`: improv vocabulary should remain searchable: Armando, La Ronde, Living Room, Mono Scene, Narrative Long-Form and Harold.
- `PRESERVED`: the useful lesson from improv is active listening, offers, status, repetition/variation and emergent structure rather than importing an improv format as a screenplay formula.
- `OUTDATED`: claims tied to one distribution era or examples presented as proof that a format belongs to only one medium.
- Formatting target: two scan-friendly lists — medium changes and improv forms.

## 33 — Why 24 Blocks Work

- `PRESERVED`: 4 acts × 6 Blocks remains the original planning resolution; PlotPickle's 12 sequences and 96 Mini-Blocks remain the current extension.
- `MODERNIZED_WITH_ALIAS`: `24 Blocks in 5-Minutes` remains the original method name and searchable historical convention.
- `PRESERVED`: the source's original approximation — roughly five minutes / five pages per Block in a roughly 120-minute / 120-page feature — should remain visible as the historical sizing heuristic, explicitly not a timing rule.
- `PRESERVED`: each Block is a manageable mini-movement that should contribute to narrative progression and character movement.
- `REJECTED`: equal duration/page count as a requirement and filler added to satisfy the grid.

## 34 — The Grid Is a Guide, Not a Cage

- `PRESERVED`: the grid remains a diagnostic surface and structural functions stay separate from page/scene execution.
- `MODERNIZED_WITH_ALIAS`: the original Structure Guide's 24 named positions should be restored as an optional reference template rather than removed as prescriptive.
- `PRESERVED`: Setup / Confrontation / Complication / Resolution is retained as the source's four-act framing vocabulary.
- `PRESERVED`: the source itself states that the map is adaptable; this flexibility should accompany the old labels.
- `REJECTED`: the 24 labels as mandatory events, mandatory Hero's Journey beats, or equal five-minute timing.
- Formatting target: four act groups with six concise Block labels each.

## 35 — Structure Creates Pressure and Choice

- `PRESERVED`: causality, objective, opposition, turn, consequence, handoff and audience orientation remain primary.
- `PRESERVED`: the source's broader functions of structure should remain explicit: cohesion/flow, pacing, character development, tension/emotional engagement and thematic expression.
- `MOVED_ELSEWHERE`: reflection/mirroring depth belongs to Lesson 40.
- `REJECTED`: the unsupported claim that conformity to a mainstream three-act structure increases a screenplay's chance of acceptance or production.
- Formatting target: a short "what structure also organizes" bullet set.

## 36 — From Beat to Act

- `PRESERVED`: beat, scene, Mini-Block, Block, sequence and act remain distinct planning resolutions.
- `MODERNIZED_WITH_ALIAS`: the detailed legacy `Story Beats` 24-position map should remain visible as a second optional reference map, not be erased because it is more prescriptive than current PlotPickle.
- `PRESERVED`: source vocabulary such as Hook, Catalyst/Inciting Incident, Philosophical Conflict, Anti-Theme, Want, Choice, Plan, Adaptation, Raised Stakes, Revelation, Crisis, All Is Lost, Dark Night of the Soul, Preparation, Climax, Fallout, New Normal and Closing Image remains useful search/craft vocabulary.
- `REJECTED`: one beat per Block as a rule, mandatory placement and a required secondary climax.
- Formatting target: 24 concise optional reference bullets grouped by the four legacy acts.

## 37 — Promise, Progress, Pressure, Payoff

- `PRESERVED`: Promise → Progress → Payoff is the source triad; Pressure remains PlotPickle's clearly labelled extension.
- `PRESERVED`: the source's fractal idea should remain explicit: the triad can be examined at act, Block, scene and beat levels.
- `PRESERVED`: replacing weak `and then` chains with causal `therefore` / `but` relationships is durable structural teaching.
- `PRESERVED`: a cycle may fit inside one Block or span multiple Blocks depending on pace and complexity.
- `REJECTED`: every act/Block/scene/beat must mechanically contain an identical three-part cycle or equal timing.

## 38 — Questions That Pull the Story Forward

- `PRESERVED`: story, act, sequence, Block and scene questions remain nested rather than flattened.
- `PRESERVED`: the source's four-act dramatic-question movement is useful as an optional example: introduce/solidify, explore/complicate, reassess/raise stakes, confront/answer/reflect.
- `MOVED_ELSEWHERE`: the source's Promise–Progress–Payoff material is taught in Lesson 37.
- `REJECTED`: fixed Block placement, 1.67-minute subdivisions and the claim that every dramatic question must be conclusively closed.
- Formatting target: four concise act-level question movements.

## 39 — One Grid, Many Story Shapes

- `PRESERVED`: presentation order versus chronology, nonlinear, parallel, episodic, circular, framed and mosaic forms remain primary.
- `MODERNIZED_WITH_ALIAS`: preserve the source's additional named vocabulary: Rashomon effect / conflicting-perspective account, split narrative, in medias res, unreliable narrator and Kishōtenketsu.
- `PRESERVED`: the 24-Block grid can organize presentation order independently from chronology.
- `REJECTED`: treating narrator reliability as a structure in every critical taxonomy or treating any named film example as proof of a universal definition.
- Formatting target: named-form glossary bullets with function rather than borrowed-film examples.

## 40 — Setup, Payoff and the Closing Image

- `PRESERVED`: setup, development, consequence, payoff, changed meaning and opening/closing reflection remain primary.
- `MODERNIZED_WITH_ALIAS`: the source's explicit four-act reflection model should remain available: Acts 1↔3 may mirror changed character/world conditions and Acts 2↔4 may echo choices and consequences.
- `PRESERVED`: a mirrored fear, relationship, object, action or situation can show change without requiring literal repetition.
- `REJECTED`: mandatory mirrored Block pairs or a requirement that every opening image have a symmetrical closing duplicate.
- Formatting target: optional reflection-pair bullets and a changed-meaning test.

## Batch result — Lessons 31–40

This batch does require restoration under the stricter standard. Earlier work correctly rejected mandatory timing and template enforcement, but it went too far when it also removed useful legacy vocabulary, named reference maps, cross-medium/improv forms, the fractal Principle of Three, advanced symbolic technique names and additional structure forms. Those elements should return as clearly labelled optional teaching while the current evidence-first, flexible PlotPickle model remains authoritative.
