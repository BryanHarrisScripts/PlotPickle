# Act I Launch

Act I Launch is PlotPickle's foundation diagnostic for Blocks 1–6. It checks whether the opening act establishes enough story information, pressure, feeling, and choice to support the remaining eighteen blocks.

It is not a fixed beat formula. The functions matter more than exact page numbers or identical scene placement. A writer can move, combine, or omit a signal when the story performs that function more effectively another way.

## Why the first six blocks matter

The opening act creates the contract that later acts develop and pay off. Before the protagonist crosses the threshold, the audience should understand:

- the world's relevant parameters and rules;
- the cast and the relationships that expose character;
- the opposition and the pressure it can apply;
- the theme in an early dramatic or visual form;
- the audience tension that keeps prediction active;
- the emotional value that can be lost; and
- the difficult decision that makes the remaining story necessary.

A launch should surprise in execution while feeling inevitable in retrospect.

## Launch contract

| Field | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Target length | Sets a pacing target for the opening without turning it into a rule. |
| Reason to flex | Explains why a familiar world may launch quickly or an unfamiliar world may need more room. |
| World contract | Records the rules and parameters the audience must understand. |
| Cast orientation | Identifies who matters now and how their roles create pressure. |
| Theme seed | Introduces the belief conflict through action, image, or relationship. |
| Audience-tension seed | Establishes the first form of the question viewers keep trying to solve. |
| Downstream promises | Lists the setups, relationships, questions, and images later acts must develop or pay off. |

## Twelve launch signals

| Block | Signal | Diagnostic function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Primary Presence | Introduces the person, force, or vulnerability that anchors attention. |
| 1 | Revealing Contrast | Uses a relationship or mirror to expose the protagonist's strength, flaw, or missing capacity. |
| 2 | Opposing Pressure | Reveals or amplifies the force capable of pushing back. |
| 2 | Pressure Multiplier | Makes the original problem more urgent, costly, or personal. |
| 3 | Disruption Lands | Overturns normal and opens a possible path with a cost. |
| 3 | Problem Named | Makes the actionable problem, fear, and consequence clear enough for the audience to state. |
| 4 | Outside Push | Uses an ally, relationship, or circumstance to push the protagonist beyond comfort. |
| 4 | Inner Lock | Shows the old strategy or flaw that still prevents full commitment. |
| 5 | Counterstrike | Lets opposition attack in a way that activates the central dramatic question. |
| 5 | Emotional Anchor | Reveals the relationship, place, value, or future that makes the goal matter. |
| 6 | Personal Threat | Puts the emotional anchor at credible risk of loss, removal, or harm. |
| 6 | Irreversible Step | Ends Act I with a choice that makes the remaining story necessary. |

The central dramatic question is the plot-level question driven by action. It should remain distinct from the theme, the protagonist's conscious want, their deeper need, and the audience-tension engine, even when those systems reinforce one another.

## Omission rule

If a signal is absent, record why. The useful question is not whether the story hits a checkpoint, but whether another scene or structural choice performs the missing function more powerfully. This keeps the grid diagnostic instead of prescriptive.

## Downstream payoff check

After Act I is mapped, review Blocks 7–24 against the downstream promises. Later acts should:

1. develop the world rules and pressures established in the launch;
2. escalate or transform the opposition;
3. test the emotional anchor and character flaw;
4. keep the central dramatic question active through visible action;
5. refresh The Pickle's audience tension; and
6. make the ending prove the change prepared in Act I.

## Workspace integration

Act I Launch is a shared story column in Instructions, Story Planner, and Visual Board. The planner stores the launch contract and twelve signals in `development.actOne`. The Visual Board shows the two relevant signals beside Blocks 1–6 and carries the downstream promises into later blocks.
