# #2094 fidelity audit — Lessons 21–30

This batch applies the stricter fidelity rule and records the first substantive restoration set.

## 21 — Map the Inner Journey Without Forcing It

- `PRESERVED`: flexible opening, catalyst/disruption, threshold/commitment, midpoint/reassessment, crisis, climax, ending and custom checkpoints remain the primary modern teaching.
- `PRESERVED`: positive, steadfast/flat, negative, tragic, recovery, ambiguous and ensemble arc possibilities remain explicit; transformation is not mandatory.
- `MODERNIZED_WITH_ALIAS`: the source's Act 1–4 / Block-specific question banks are restored as a flexible movement/checkpoint question bank rather than compulsory placement.
- `MODERNIZED_WITH_ALIAS`: familiar labels such as `All Is Lost`, `Dark Night of the Soul` and `secondary climax` remain recognizable as traditions while being explicitly non-required.
- `REJECTED`: mandatory Block placement, mandatory rock-bottom beats, one fixed ending pattern and compulsory positive transformation.
- Formatting improved through 20 scan-friendly question bullets grouped by dramatic purpose.

## 22 — Join Inner and Outer Conflict

- `PRESERVED`: the neutral pressure spectrum remains primary: self; person/relationship; group/institution/system; environment/nature; technology/constructed system; time/history/fate/social expectation.
- `MODERNIZED_WITH_ALIAS`: `Man vs Man`, `Man vs Self/Himself`, `Man vs Nature`, `Man vs Machine` and `Man vs Other/Society` are restored as traditional/search terms beside modern neutral labels.
- `PRESERVED`: durable subtypes from the old conflict sources are restored: family/rivalry/power; fear/self-doubt/moral dilemma/self-worth; environment/scarcity; technology/dependency/control; societal norms and group pressure.
- `REJECTED`: machines as inherently emotionless or superior, gendered labels as current preferred terminology, and conflict category as a complete theme by itself.
- Formatting improved with a dedicated alias section and subtype bullets.

## 23 — Build Opposition as a Competing Worldview

- `PRESERVED`: opposing forces retain objectives, agency, self-justification, evidence and the ability to initiate action.
- `MODERNIZED_WITH_ALIAS`: `thesis`, `antithesis` and `synthesis` are restored explicitly as traditional dialectical vocabulary while remaining an optional lens.
- `PRESERVED`: antagonist, rival and foil distinctions are now explicit rather than relying on the learner to infer their difference.
- `REJECTED`: forced synthesis, simple good/evil equivalence and an antagonist who exists only to block the protagonist.
- Formatting improved through definitions and comparison bullets.

## 24 — Design Relationships That Change Both People

- `PRESERVED`: reciprocal wants, assigned roles, disputed history, affection/dependence/debt/resentment, status/power, withholding, tactics and turning events remain primary.
- `PRESERVED`: source relationship teaching is restored explicitly as optional lenses: chemistry, mentor–pupil, loyalty/betrayal, family/chosen family, companionship/rivalry, power dynamics, shared experience and repair.
- `MODERNIZED_WITH_ALIAS`: recognizable relationship terms remain available without turning them into formulas or fixed outcomes.
- Formatting improved through one relationship-lens section and eight explanatory bullets.

## 25 — Give Every Character a Playable Voice

- `PRESERVED`: worldview, attention, rhythm, sentence shape, vocabulary, metaphor, verbal fingerprints, humour/avoidance, emotional access, status, persuasion, silence and action remain explicit.
- `PRESERVED`: Character Guide factors are restored explicitly: origin/environment, education/expertise, profession/role, personality/worldview, relationship/status and pressure.
- `REJECTED`: accent, ethnicity, class or demographic category as sufficient voice design; phonetic caricature; AI as authenticity certification.
- Formatting improved through a seven-point source-influence checklist.

## 26 — Design the Cast as a Dramatic System

- `PRESERVED`: PlotPickle's function-first cast model remains primary and characters may combine, transfer or reverse functions.
- `MODERNIZED_WITH_ALIAS`: traditional archetype vocabulary is restored for discoverability — Hero, Mentor, Villain/Antagonist, Antihero, Sidekick/Ally, Trickster/Jester, Shapeshifter, Outcast, Everyman, Herald, Threshold Guardian, Shadow and the historical `Damsel` / rescue label — while clearly separating terms from identity boxes.
- `PRESERVED`: the durable Character-pattern library is restored as optional generative lenses: sacrifice/adversity, competing drives, arc possibilities, hero-position variations, relationship patterns, status/community patterns and tonal/intellectual contrasts.
- `PRESERVED`: minor-character function, stereotype avoidance, memorable entrances/exits and non-human-character craft are now explicit.
- `PRESERVED`: non-human characters retain coherent drives, abilities, limitations, relationships and consequences while preserving meaningful difference rather than simply humanizing everything.
- `OUTDATED`: profitability rankings and box-office proof attached to old character examples.
- `REJECTED`: required archetype roster, fixed Hero/Mentor/Villain interactions, gendered rescue role as normative casting and automatic merging of apparently redundant characters.
- Formatting improved through three distinct sections with grouped bullets instead of reproducing the source's long article sequence.

## 27 — Mood, Colour and Visual Language

- `PRESERVED`: mood versus tone, palette, hue/value/saturation/contrast, lighting, texture, composition, camera feel, repetition/variation and visual continuity are explicit.
- `PRESERVED`: reference boards remain vocabulary/research surfaces rather than automatic canon or generated assets.
- `DUPLICATE`: the very large Mood Board Collection is primarily a gallery/index of visual examples and generated image descriptions; its volume is reference evidence, not thousands of words of missing theory.
- `MOVED_ELSEWHERE`: early-production application of reference material belongs to Visual Storytelling / visual development.
- Formatting is already structured around visual dimensions, definitions and application.

## 28 — Theme, motif and foreshadowing pass

- `PRESERVED`: this is a bounded diagnostic pass for evidence, recurrence, setup/payoff, ambiguity and human approval rather than a second full theme lesson.
- `MOVED_ELSEWHERE`: full theme argument teaching belongs to Lesson 30 and full motif/symbol craft belongs to Lesson 31.
- `REJECTED`: declaring one objectively correct theme, inserting symbolism everywhere, over-signalling twists or automatically removing ambiguity.
- No expansion required.

## 29 — Design Tone Without Monotony

- `PRESERVED`: tone is the work's attitude toward people, events and consequences; mood remains the local emotional atmosphere.
- `PRESERVED`: diction, image, sound, rhythm, point of view and consequence can maintain tonal identity while scenes move through different emotional registers.
- `REJECTED`: consistency as one unchanging mood or genre as a fixed tonal speed.
- No additional source-depth restoration required.

## 30 — Make Theme a Dramatic Argument

- `PRESERVED`: theme is tested through plot, character belief, conflict, choice and consequence rather than announced as a slogan.
- `PRESERVED`: multiple, complementary and competing interpretations of one thematic question are explicit.
- `PRESERVED`: subtle expression through action and consequence retains the durable source teaching about avoiding preachiness.
- `MOVED_ELSEWHERE`: symbolism and recurring visual reinforcement belong to Lesson 31.
- `REJECTED`: every event/dialogue line must repeat one moral message; every character must positively transform to prove the theme.
- Formatting is already suited to argument/counterargument/evidence rather than requiring expansion.

## Batch result — Lessons 21–30

Lessons 21–26 required genuine restoration under the stricter standard and have been expanded without replacing current terminology or authority. Lessons 27–30 remain high-fidelity; their apparent raw-source word volume is largely reference material, duplication or teaching deliberately owned by neighbouring lessons.
