# Voiceprint Engine

The Voiceprint Engine makes dialogue a consequence of character, relationship, pressure, and story world rather than a layer added after plotting.

## Purpose

Every major character should sound recognizable without relying on names or dialogue tags. PlotPickle accomplishes this by connecting speech decisions to the same character and block data already used by Story Planner and Visual Board.

The engine is built around four questions:

1. Who formed this speaker?
2. Who are they speaking to right now?
3. What do they want from that person?
4. What changes in their speech when pressure rises?

## Character voiceprint

A character voiceprint records the influences that create a repeatable speaking pattern:

- Origin and environment: geography, community, family, work, culture, and formative surroundings.
- Social context: class, status, money, institutions, belonging, and exclusion.
- Education and expertise: formal education, practical knowledge, professional language, and blind spots.
- Worldview and boundaries: beliefs, loyalties, taboos, assumptions, and subjects they avoid.
- Rhythm and sentence shape: clipped or expansive, fast or deliberate, direct or circling.
- Vocabulary and metaphors: preferred words, references, comparisons, and areas of fluency.
- Verbal fingerprints: repeated connectors, fillers, corrections, jokes, deflections, and signature phrasing.
- Emotional access: what can be said plainly, what is disguised, and what cannot be admitted.
- Status shift: how the voice changes with authority, intimacy, fear, attraction, rivalry, or dependence.
- Persuasion strategy: how the character asks, pressures, charms, bargains, threatens, proves, or avoids.

The existing compact Voice field remains the summary rule. The voiceprint fields explain why that rule works and make it easier to apply consistently.

## Scene-level dialogue logic

Dialogue is generated or revised from the intersection of four inputs:

- Character identity: the stored voiceprint.
- Relationship: the specific history and power balance between speakers.
- Scene objective: what each speaker wants from the exchange.
- Pressure: the emotional, practical, or social cost of failing.

The same character may sound different with a parent, stranger, employee, rival, doctor, or lover, but the differences should still feel like variations of one person.

## Story-world language

Each project can define a world vernacular: the shared vocabulary, slang, rituals, technical terms, forms of address, and speech rules created by the setting and genre.

World language should unify the story without flattening individual voices. Characters may share terminology while differing in rhythm, confidence, precision, humour, metaphor, and emotional openness.

## Monologues

A long speech must be earned by story pressure. It should not exist only because the writer has something impressive to say.

A useful monologue normally does at least one of the following:

- persuades another character;
- defends a choice;
- reframes evidence;
- makes an emotional bid;
- exposes a contradiction;
- changes the power or direction of the scene.

PlotPickle treats a monologue as an argument with an intended effect, even when only one person speaks for an extended period.

## Subtext

Subtext becomes effective after the audience understands the immediate situation and the characters involved. Clarity creates the foundation for indirection.

PlotPickle tracks subtext through:

- the desire a character cannot state directly;
- objects or actions that carry relationship meaning;
- words whose meaning changes through repetition;
- callbacks that gain emotional weight;
- avoidance patterns that reveal the subject beneath the subject.

Subtext should increase tension or meaning, not make basic story information unnecessarily confusing.

## Exposition

Exposition is necessary whenever the audience needs information to understand a choice, danger, rule, or consequence. The goal is not to eliminate exposition but to prevent it from sounding like the writer temporarily speaking through a character.

Strong exposition is delivered through character activity:

- disagreement;
- persuasion;
- humour;
- urgency;
- emotional memory;
- a practical task;
- conflicting interpretations;
- consequences unfolding in the scene.

Information should be attached to something a character wants now.

## Observation practice

Writers can build a voice library by observing real speech ethically and respectfully. The goal is not imitation of a complete person. It is the study of small fingerprints: sentence length, hesitation, repetition, favourite comparisons, topic changes, emotional deflection, and status shifts.

Those observations should be recombined into original fictional voices rather than copied as recognizable portraits.

## PlotPickle integration

The Voiceprint Engine belongs to the canonical project object and connects to:

- Characters: stores identity, history, and speech fingerprints.
- Dialogue: stores project-wide rules for vernacular, monologues, subtext, exposition, and observation.
- Relationships: explains how a voice changes with a specific person.
- 24 Blocks: supplies scene goal, conflict, choice, pressure, and story text.
- Visual Board: supports performance notes, visible reactions, interruptions, and status changes.

The result is one dialogue system shared across planning, writing, revision, and eventual AI-assisted workflows without making AI a requirement.
