## Result

All ten Phase 4 lessons were learner-sufficient before changes. No learner-facing curriculum rewrite was justified.

The useful teaching in the Phase 4 bundled sources is already present in normal lesson sections or has an explicit current owner. Material that would make the curriculum more rigid, dated or misleading remains rejected instead of being copied forward.

### Theme / motif

`Build Motifs, Symbols and Echoes` already teaches motif, symbol, metaphor, rhyme, foreshadowing, setup/payoff, irony, allegorical design and opening/closing echo through context, repetition and changed meaning. Fixed symbol dictionaries and universal colour/object meanings remain rejected. Dialogue subtext and broader visual-storytelling craft remain owned by their dedicated lessons rather than duplicated here.

### Structure and 24 Blocks

`Story Structures: Screenplays to Improv` already explains structure as an arrangement of pressure, choice, consequence and revelation; compares multiple structural traditions; covers nonlinear, parallel, circular, framed and episodic forms; and maps structural turns to the 24-Block planning grid without forcing a single theory.

`Why 24 Blocks Work` and `The Grid Is a Guide, Not a Cage` already preserve the durable core of the original 24-Blocks material: Blocks are manageable planning movements, the four-act/six-Block layout is a useful visual resolution, scene count and length remain flexible, and the grid is diagnostic rather than prescriptive. Personal origin/history remains provenance only. Fixed five-minute/five-page timing and prescribed Block events remain rejected.

`Structure Creates Pressure and Choice` already teaches causality, objective/opposition/turn/consequence and audience orientation. The reflection/setup-payoff material attached through its historical source is more appropriately taught by Lesson 40. Unsupported claims that conformity to a standard structure increases production acceptance are not promoted.

`From Beat to Act` already distinguishes beat, scene, mini-block, Block, sequence and act, with links between planning scales. The historical source's one-beat-per-Block prescription, fixed timing and mandatory All Is Lost / Dark Night / secondary-climax sequence are not current authority.

`Promise, Progress, Pressure, Payoff` already preserves the original Promise–Progress–Payoff triad and clearly labels Pressure as PlotPickle's extension. Mechanical repetition of the same triad at every scale remains rejected.

`Questions That Pull the Story Forward` already teaches nested story/act/sequence/Block/scene questions and deliberate question handoff. The source's fixed act/block placements and 1.67-minute subdivision are not promoted.

`One Grid, Many Story Shapes` already teaches presentation order versus chronology and adapts the grid to nonlinear, parallel, episodic, circular, framed and mosaic forms. Named-film examples and the source's looser classification of devices such as unreliable narration are not required for the learner to understand the durable structural principle.

`Setup, Payoff and the Closing Image` has no bundled source attached to its presentation record, but it already contains the durable reflection teaching routed to it by the prior source-drain work: setup, development, consequence, payoff, changed meaning and opening/closing-image relationship.
