## Era 1 — Afterglow on the page

Bryan Harris wrote the screenplay that became Afterglow in 2017. Beats, outlines, pitch material, screenplay drafts and later visual experiments accumulated across files and repositories. The initial public experiment asked whether an unproduced screenplay could remain useful as writing, learning material and a source for visual exploration.

**Retained:** Afterglow as a built-in example, its source history, the 24-Block storyboard, mood references and current-versus-legacy comparison.

**Revised:** The user-facing title is `Afterglow: Reflections of Sentience`; rights, source and generated-asset status are recorded explicitly rather than inferred from repository visibility.

## Era 2 — 24 Blocks as a common story language

The screenplay was divided into twenty-four dramatic movements so a writer could focus on one manageable unit while retaining awareness of the whole story. The structure became a shared vocabulary across story architecture, character, dialogue, images and experiments with AI.

**Retained:** four editable Acts, twelve Sequences, twenty-four Blocks, flexible Scenes, ninety-six mini-blocks, beats and shots.

**Revised:** the hierarchy is human-readable first and AI-compatible rather than AI-dependent. Five minutes per Block and a two-hour feature remain editable reference presets, not rules.

## Era 3 — OpenStory Studio experiments

GitHub hosted learning articles, screenplay resources, mood boards, logline prompts and public collaboration experiments. Separate GPT products—Architect, Plus and Visualizer—tested different front ends. Community, social-publishing, web3, token, DAO, job-board and micropayment ideas were explored. A proposed CrewAI architecture imagined several specialized agents coordinating script segmentation and visualization.

**Retained:** specialized responsibilities, learning paths, visual references, prompt preparation, version history and the idea that unseen scripts deserve a useful visual-development path.

**Consolidated:** Architect became Story Setup, World, Locations, Visual Bible and Research & Canon. Plus became Story Planner, Screenplay, Character, Dialogue, Read & Learn and Engines. Visualizer became Visual Board, storyboards, shots and Production.

**Retired from the current direction:** required GPT subscriptions, web3, blockchain, NFTs, tokens, DAO governance, micropayment and revenue promises, open-ended public editing, autonomous-agent core architecture and claims of automatic script interpretation or finished-film production.

## Era 4 — One connected local application

PlotPickle replaced the separate front ends with one downloadable local-server application. Story foundations, structure, treatment, screenplay, visuals, review, production, rights and provenance now read and write one portable canonical project.

The current page-to-production path is:

`Idea → Story Setup → Character and World → 24 Blocks → Flexible Scenes → 96 Mini-Blocks → Treatment → Screenplay → Review → Visual Board → Shots and Production → Pitch and Export`

This is a connected workflow, not a claim that PlotPickle automatically creates a movie or guarantees an audience, production, employment or revenue.

## Era 5 — Writer-controlled collaboration and extensibility

Portable `.ppf` projects, rolling backups, revision history, rights records, owner-controlled GitHub proposals, provider-independent AI, plugin contracts and a public SDK extend PlotPickle without making cloud services mandatory.

Current collaboration is independent local work plus bounded, reviewable proposals. Real-time simultaneous editing is deferred. GitHub is optional infrastructure rather than a writing requirement.

## What the legacy work taught

### Common language helps people and tools communicate

The 24 Blocks remain useful because they let writers, collaborators, screenplay elements, visuals and optional AI refer to the same dramatic location. Uncertainty and imported interpretations are marked as proposals rather than facts.

### Large stories become manageable at several resolutions

Whole concept, Acts, Sequences, Blocks, Scenes, mini-blocks, beats and shots answer different questions. Writers can move among them without losing the canonical story.

### Unproduced scripts still have value

A screenplay can remain valuable as writing, learning material, a collaboration source, a visual-development project or a production plan even when it has not yet been produced.

### Technology must remain human-led

Generative systems can add language, structures and ideas. PlotPickle does not pretend otherwise. The safeguards are deliberate context, transparent provenance, human evaluation, retained originals and explicit approval.

### Personal history and product guidance are different

Bryan Harris's personal manifestos, family values, gratitude and creative history remain part of the origins record. General product guidance uses the stable principle: **The writer's decisions define the work.** Personal mental-health self-guidance is not presented as a universal software philosophy.

## Licensing evolution

The reusable 24 Blocks method and identified educational material may be shared under CC BY-SA 4.0. PlotPickle software uses AGPL-3.0-or-later. User-created stories are not automatically licensed. Afterglow-specific rights, third-party material, trademarks and generated assets require their own recorded terms.

Legacy statements tied to old dates or blanket legal conclusions are historical only. Users should verify current jurisdiction-specific copyright, contract, union and AI rules.
